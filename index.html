Application Quick Guide

Overview of features, filters, and controls

🗺️

3D Globe & Interactive Map

Explore seismic events in full 3D orthographic projection. Drag to pan and rotate the globe, scroll or pinch to zoom. Use the Layers menu (top right of map) to choose basemaps (Light, Dark, Street, Satellite, Terrain) and toggle tectonic plates or the aerial survey.

🔴

Magnitude & Focal Depth Colors

Marker size reflects Earthquake Magnitude. Colors represent Focal Depth from shallow to deep (Green <10km → Blue 10–30km → Yellow 30–50km → Orange 50–70km → Red 70–90km → Dark red >90km). Click any marker to view time, depth, magnitude, and tsunami notices.

🔍

Search & Time Range Filters

Switch between 1h, 24h, 7d, 30d feeds or choose custom historical periods. Search by city, region, or country (e.g. Japan, Chile, California), or tap Min Mag chips to filter out lower magnitudes.

⚡

Feed List & Epicenter Fly-To

Click Feed List in the toolbar to slide out the activity list. Click any earthquake entry to automatically fly directly to its coordinates and pop open its detailed event card.

▶️

Interactive Timelapse Playback

Click Timelapse to replay earthquake occurrences step-by-step chronologically across your chosen time range. Scrub the progress bar or pause at any moment to analyze clusters.

🚨

PAGER Alerts & Data Export

Assess real-world earthquake impact with official USGS PAGER alert badges (Green, Yellow, Orange, Red) and Mercalli Intensity (MMI) indicators. Export your current filtered earthquake view to CSV or GeoJSON anytime via the Feed List header.

⛏️

Human-Induced vs. Natural Quakes

Filter between natural tectonic events and human-induced seismicity (quarry blasts, mining explosions, rock bursts). Human-induced tremors trigger custom explosive acoustic sonification and amber alert badges.

🧭

Quake Quest: Learn Geology by Playing

Tap any quake and you'll get a field question first: how deep did it start, what kind of plate boundary made it, or how much energy did it release? Answer, then the facts are revealed with a short lesson. Got it wrong? Ask the tutor why and Claude explains, using that quake's real data. Every quake you predict is logged in your Field Guide, daily missions send you hunting across today's map, badges mark milestones, and you command your own survey airships: choose your first, then add one with every promotion (or sooner, by a ship's deed). Each carries an instrument that finds a kind of quake you haven't predicted yet: tap 📡 Scan on the map to ring them and fly to each one. Open your Field Journal (🧭 at the top) to switch between Cadet and Scientist wording or turn the questions off. Quake Quest is played with your Emberkite tag (one gamer tag for every Emberkite game): sign in or claim one from 🧭, and any progress already on this device comes along.

🛸

Aerial Survey Fleet & Regional Depth Pings

Track the Victorian retro-fantasy dirigible fleet — nine ships in three squadrons — orbiting the planet between the major parallels. Each flagship leads two smaller escort ships patrolling between the Arctic Circle, the tropics, and the Antarctic Circle. When new earthquakes are detected in their region, the whole squadron blows its unique steam whistle and pings its bowsprit searchlights with the earthquake's depth color (Green <10km → Blue/Yellow/Orange 10–70km → Red/Crimson >70km):

  • 🛸 HMS Aetheria (No. 1894-A) Northern Squadron (36°N) with escorts HMS Borealis (55°N) & HMS Cirrus (22°N) · 3-Chime Steam Whistle (D Major brass chord).
  • 🛸 HMS Equinox (No. 1894-B) Equatorial Squadron (0°) with escorts HMS Zephyrine (10°N) & HMS Monsoon (10°S) · Clarion Fluted Siren (E Major melodic chime).
  • 🛸 HMS Australis (No. 1894-C) Southern Squadron (35°S) with escorts HMS Notus (22°S) & HMS Antipodes (52°S) · Dreadnought Foghorn (G Minor deep resonant roar).

Click any ship or the HMS Aetheria button to inspect live 3D models, flight telemetry, and test each squadron's authentic whistle and depth beacon.
